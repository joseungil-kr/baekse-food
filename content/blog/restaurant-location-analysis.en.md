<h2 class="h2">Location Determines Half of a Restaurant's Success</h2>
<p class="lead">Is a location good just because it has a lot of foot traffic? The answer is 'no'. A truly good location is one where the target customer base that matches the item stays.</p>

<h3 class="h3" style="margin-top:32px">3 Trap Locations to Avoid</h3>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Commuter Transit Locations:</strong> Right in front of a subway station, foot traffic is overflowing, but people only pass by hurriedly without lingering. Efficiency is extremely low compared to the high rent.</li>
  <li><strong>Overly Office-Specialized Locations:</strong> Weekday lunch business is booming, but it turns into a ghost town in the evenings or on weekends. If you can't supplement evening and weekend sales, it's best to avoid.</li>
  <li><strong>Single-Item Dense Areas:</strong> In the center of highly competitive food alleys, excessive marketing costs and cutthroat competition make it difficult for beginners to survive.</li>
</ul>

<h3 class="h3" style="margin-top:32px">Optimal Recommended Locations for Baekse Food</h3>
<p>Baekse Food features a 3-in-1 hybrid structure that <strong>rotates 3 times a day</strong>: 'Barley Bibimbap' during the day, and 'Dakhanmari' and 'Baekse Pocha' in the evening.</p>
<p>Therefore, the most recommended location is a <strong>mixed commercial area combining offices and residential zones</strong>. You can serve hearty meals to office workers on weekday lunches, and stably attract family dining and company gatherings on evenings and weekends. Even in lower-rent B-grade backstreet locations rather than expensive A-grade main street locations, high profits comparable to A-grade spots can be achieved by combining delivery and takeout—this is Baekse Food's powerful weapon.</p>
